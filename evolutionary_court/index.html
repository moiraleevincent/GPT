The Case of the Helpful Relative

A defender of evolution is called to account for a rather awkward fact: some animals help their relatives at a cost to themselves. Two fictional presenters investigate kin altruism, inclusive fitness, Hamilton's rule, and what an evolutionary explanation can—and cannot—tell us.

Based on: the opening pages of Chapter 7, “The Evolutionary Psychology of Social Behaviour: Kin Relationships and Conflict,” photographed by the listener. This is an independently written teaching adaptation, not a recording of the text.

The characters and voices are synthetic. Animal observations are distinct from claims about their ultimate causes. The numerical example is illustrative, not a measurement from a study.
