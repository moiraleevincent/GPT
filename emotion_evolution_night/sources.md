# The Social Lives of Emotions — source ledger (researched 27 October 2026)

This is a new one-voice, sleep-friendly, research-led evolutionary psychology audio essay. Each claim in the spoken script should distinguish **observation / experiment** from **adaptive interpretation**, and avoid adaptationist just-so certainty. No personal anecdotes about the listener.

## Central theory, epistemic boundaries
- Nesse (1990), *Evolutionary explanations of emotions*, Human Nature. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24222085/ — specialized states in recurrent situations, with possible design features; older conceptual foundation.
- 2025 review *Basic Emotions or Constructed Emotions: Insights From Taking an Evolutionary Perspective*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12065949/ — distinguishes evolutionary function, mechanism, subjective feeling; contrasting schools remain open.
- Al-Shawaf & Shackelford (eds.), *Oxford Handbook of Evolution and the Emotions* (May 2024). https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/56886 — broad cross-disciplinary synthesis; proximate vs ultimate questions.

## Anger
- Sell et al. (2017), *The grammar of anger: Mapping the computational architecture of a recalibrational emotion*, Cognition. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28668649/ — 23 experiments, six cultures, cost/benefit to offender and being specifically targeted modulate anger. The evolutionary **interpretation** is an anger recalibration / welfare-tradeoff model.
- Sell, Tooby & Cosmides (2009), *Formidability and the logic of human anger*, PNAS. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19666613/ — tested predictions linking physical formidability / bargaining position to anger and entitlement. Note correlational and interpretive limits.

## Shame and embarrassment
- Sznycer et al. (2016), *Shame closely tracks the threat of devaluation by others, even across cultures*, PNAS. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26903649/ — correlations .69 USA, .79 India, .67 Israel; scenario-based shame vs audience devaluation.
- Sznycer et al. (2018), *Cross-cultural invariances in the architecture of shame*, PNAS. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30201711/ — 899 people, 15 small-scale communities, mean r .84 between shame and local audience devaluation.
- Argaman et al. (2026), *Cross-cultural evidence that shame is a defense against reputational damage*, PNAS. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41871240/ — public > private failures; major > minor failures; tests in six countries + US regions. Note measures are responses to **hypothetical scenarios**, not observation of evolved history.
- Feinberg, Willer & Keltner (2012), *Flustered and faithful: embarrassment as a signal of prosociality*, JPSP. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21928915/ — five studies, displays of embarrassment led observers to infer prosociality, trust and affiliate more. Do not claim involuntary blushing makes all signals unfakeable.

## Guilt and repair
- *The social function of the feeling and expression of guilt* (2021). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33489253/ — induced guilt promoted repair effort, but observing guilt *did not simply reduce punishment*: close friends punished a guilty friend more than an acquaintance in this paradigm. This complexity makes a compelling scene.
- *When It's Good to Feel Bad: An Evolutionary Model of Guilt and Apology*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7805885/ — evolutionary GAME THEORY model of guilty apology and repeated cooperation; a model, not direct ancestral proof.
- Zhang, Zeelenberg & Breugelmans (2024), *Examining the role of relational utility in guilt and regret*, Acta Psychologica. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691824004852 — relational utility variation and guilt.

## Pride / status
- Sznycer et al. (2017), *Cross-cultural regularities in the cognitive architecture of pride*, PNAS. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28167752/ — tests of how pride tracks anticipated social valuation.
- *Pride: The Emotional Foundation of Social Rank Attainment* (2023), Annual Review of Psychology. https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-psych-032720-040321 — authentic/hubristic pride; prestige and dominance, as proposed interpretations.

## Gratitude and compassion
- Algoe (2012), *Find, Remind, and Bind*, Social and Personality Psychology Compass. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2012.00439.x — gratitude proposed as relationship-detection and strengthening.
- Gordon et al. (2012), *To have and to hold*, JPSP. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22642482/ — multi-method relationship appreciation research.
- Goetz & Simon-Thomas (2024), *Compassion: An Evolutionary Account*, Oxford Handbook. https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/56886/chapter-abstract/455230216 — nurturance and cooperation model.

## Disgust and threat
- Cepon-Robins (2024), *Disgust: An Emotion for Pathogen Avoidance*, Oxford Handbook. https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/56886/chapter-abstract/455226983 — behavioral immune system, substantial limitations in WEIRD-heavy evidence.
- Tybur et al. (2018), *Why do people vary in disgust?*, Phil Trans B. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29866917/ — three common explanations for variability not supported by existing data according to review.
- Frynta et al. (2023), *Are vipers prototypic fear-evoking snakes?*, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37928591/ — Somali/Czech comparison.
- Luck et al. (2020), *Prepared fear or socio-cultural learning?*, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31828815/ — instructed extinction challenges strong snake/spider prepared-fear account.

## Jealousy, loss, awe
- *Jealousy in Close Relationships From an Evolutionary and Cultural Perspective* (2023), Oxford Handbook. https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/45634/chapter-abstract/396141657 — distinguish possessive vs reactive; individual / cultural conditions; no justification for controlling behaviors.
- *Why are some people more jealous than others? Genetic and environmental factors* (2022), Evolution and Human Behavior. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1090513821000611 — Finnish twin/sibling study, 29% heritability in that sample; not individual determinism.
- Mikulincer & Shaver (2022), *An attachment perspective on loss and grief*. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35008028/ — attachment theory, grief and adaptation.
- Forgas (2024), *The Evolutionary Functions of Sadness*, Oxford Handbook. https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/56886/chapter-abstract/455229721 — sadness may facilitate externally focused, careful thinking in some experimental tasks.
- Jiang et al. (2024), *The unique nature and psychosocial implications of awe*, Nature Reviews Psychology. https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-024-00322-z — nuanced effects on the self and methodological limits.
