01 / Action and feedback

The two gulfs

When you approach an unfamiliar device, there are two questions. First: what can I do, and how do I do it? That is the gulf of execution. Second: what happened after I acted, and did it accomplish what I wanted? That is the gulf of evaluation. Good design narrows both distances. It makes the possible actions clear, and the results easy to understand.

02 / Self-blame and design

Blaming the wrong things

You turn a control, wait, and nothing seems to happen. You try again. This time, perhaps, you wonder whether you misunderstood something obvious. But when people repeatedly struggle with an ordinary object, the important question is not whether they are clever enough to use it. It is whether the design has given them enough information to succeed.
