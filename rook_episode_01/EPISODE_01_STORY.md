# THE HOUSE OF ALMOST
## Episode One — A Very Unfortunate Theft
**Story draft · 16 November 2026**

The trouble began with a horse.

It was a very small horse, made of painted porcelain, with one leg shorter than the others and an expression of mild personal disappointment.

Rook stole it because it looked lonely.

She had come to Lord Bellamy’s annual ball to steal something considerably more valuable.

Lady Bellamy’s sapphire brooch, to be precise.

A gentleman in the city had offered her eighty gold pieces for it. Eighty was enough to pay her landlord, settle two unfortunate debts, and buy a new pair of boots. Rook was particularly interested in the boots.

She wore a stolen blue dress and a smile she had practiced in the mirror approximately four minutes before arriving.

The evening had been rather successful so far. She had taken seventeen gold coins from a drunk, a silver spoon, and an astonishingly ugly ring from a woman who had called her “dear girl.”

But the brooch was proving difficult.

Lady Bellamy had spent the entire evening surrounded by people, most of whom seemed determined to kiss her hand. Rook had made three approaches and been interrupted each time by a man who wanted to discuss horses.

Actual horses.

When she finally got close enough to see the sapphire, something bothered her.

It was beautiful, certainly. Huge and blue and surrounded by diamonds.

But under the candlelight, the blue stone looked strangely flat.

Rook leaned in.

“Your brooch is magnificent.”

Lady Bellamy beamed.

“A family heirloom.”

“How wonderful. May I?”

Without waiting for permission, Rook lifted the jewel gently between two fingers.

Glass.

Very good glass, but glass nonetheless.

“Well,” Rook said, letting it drop back against the woman’s chest. “How disappointing.”

Lady Bellamy frowned. “I beg your pardon?”

“The champagne. Terrible year.”

Rook fled toward the dessert table.

Someone had stolen the real sapphire before she had even begun.

That was when she noticed the man with the spectacles.

He was thin, carefully dressed, and standing beside a pillar, staring directly at her handbag.

Rook checked her dress. Nothing amiss. Checked the bag. Coins, spoon, ring, little porcelain horse.

He looked away a fraction too late.

Interesting.

She took a strawberry, dipped it in chocolate, and walked over.

“Lovely party.”

The gentleman said nothing.

“Do I know you?”

“No.”

“Then we’re off to a splendid start.”

His eyes dropped to her bag.

“I believe you've picked up something that doesn't belong to you.”

“That does narrow it down remarkably little.”

“The horse.”

Rook looked offended.

“You're accusing me of stealing a horse?”

“I saw you take it.”

“Then why on earth did you ask?”

He extended his hand. “Give it back.”

Rook considered him. His immaculate collar. His trembling fingers. The little bead of sweat creeping down the side of his face.

She had never seen a man so frightened over such an ugly ornament.

“Twenty gold,” she said.

“Absolutely not.”

“Thirty, then. You've been disagreeable.”

He caught her wrist.

“Listen carefully. If anyone discovers what you're carrying—”

Rook twisted sideways, neatly slipped two fingers into his waistcoat pocket, and felt a small brass key.

“—you will put us both in considerable danger,” he finished.

Rook took the key.

Then she kicked him in the shin.

The gentleman gave a strangled cry. Rook backed away.

Unfortunately, she backed directly into a servant carrying a tray of glasses.

There was an enormous crash.

Her handbag hit the floor.

The porcelain horse flew out, bounced once, and broke against the marble.

For a moment, everyone stared.

Someone at the back of the ballroom shouted, “WHO BROUGHT A HORSE?”

Rook knelt to retrieve the fragments.

Something blue glittered inside the broken body.

Not paint.

A sapphire.

A magnificent, enormous sapphire, still attached to its gold brooch.

She looked up.

The gentleman was looking down at her with the expression of a man who had just watched his entire future fall onto the floor.

“Oh,” Rook said.

“Don't.”

“Oh, you clever little worm.”

“Be quiet.”

“You stole it.”

He crouched beside her. “I swapped it before the ball. The horse was meant to leave with the catering.”

“And then?”

“And then you put it in your handbag!”

Rook smiled.

“I'm terribly sorry for improving your plan.”

He reached for the jewel. Rook closed her hand over it.

“You are going to give me that,” he hissed.

“Am I?”

“Yes.”

“See, that's usually when people start offering money.”

Before he could answer, a bell rang.

Lord Bellamy had climbed onto the musicians' platform. He held the false brooch between two fingers.

“Someone has replaced my wife's sapphire with a piece of glass!”

The room went silent.

Rook and the gentleman looked at one another.

“Lock the doors!” Lord Bellamy roared.

Four servants hurried toward the exits.

Rook slipped the jewel into her palm and stood up.

“Well,” she said. “That's inconvenient.”

The gentleman seized her elbow.

“My name is Quill,” he said. “I have a key to the garden gate. We can get out through the terrace, provided you do exactly as I say.”

“Mr. Quill. Delighted.”

“The brooch. Half each.”

“Seventy-thirty.”

“Half.”

“Sixty-forty.”

“Half!”

Rook sighed.

“Fine. Half.”

“Good. Now give me my key.”

Rook opened her other hand.

The brass key lay across her palm.

Mr. Quill stared.

“WHEN DID YOU—”

“Shh. We're supposed to be discreet.”

A guard was making his way through the guests, examining their bags.

Quill pulled Rook behind the dessert table.

“There is no way he'll let us out,” he whispered.

Rook peeked over the table.

She watched the guard stop a young woman, inspect her purse, and wave her back into the crowd. Then he moved toward them.

Rook looked at Quill.

“Can you catch?”

“What?”

“Can you catch a woman?”

“Why would I—”

Rook staggered out from behind the table.

“Oh, darling!”

Quill went rigid.

“My heart,” she cried. “My poor, poor heart!”

She collapsed into his arms.

Quill tried to catch her. He succeeded, technically, though both his knees bent at a deeply alarming angle.

A woman gasped.

“She's fainted!”

“Fetch a physician!”

“Fresh air!” shouted Rook, in the weak voice of a woman apparently at death's door. “I need fresh air!”

The guard hesitated at the terrace doors.

Lord Bellamy was still shouting about the sapphire.

An elderly duchess struck the guard on the arm with her fan.

“For heaven's sake, man! Open the door!”

He opened it.

Quill half-carried, half-dragged Rook onto the terrace.

The moment the doors closed behind them, she stood upright.

“Excellent work.”

Quill doubled over, wheezing.

“You are a terrible woman.”

“Thank you.”

They crossed the wet garden toward a locked iron gate. Quill held out his hand.

“The key.”

Rook gave it to him.

He unlocked the gate and pulled it open.

They were free.

For a moment, both stood there in the rain, listening to the muffled uproar inside the ballroom.

Then Quill straightened his collar.

“Now. My half.”

Rook looked at him.

“Your half of what?”

“The sapphire!”

“Oh, Mr. Quill.” She sounded wounded. “You were doing so well.”

He lunged for her handbag.

He found a spoon, an ugly ring, seventeen coins, and the broken porcelain horse, which Rook had collected on the way out.

No brooch.

Quill stared at the pieces.

Rook picked up a chocolate-covered strawberry from the fold of her sleeve.

She peeled back the chocolate. The sapphire gleamed inside.

Quill's mouth fell open.

“You put it in the strawberry?”

“While you were busy shouting at me.”

“You agreed to half!”

“I agreed to half of a plan. You provided the gate. I provided the fainting.”

“That is NOT—”

Rook popped the strawberry into her mouth.

Her expression changed immediately.

She stopped chewing.

Quill watched.

“Miss Rook?”

Rook coughed.

“Did you just swallow the—”

She coughed again, bent forward, and spat the sapphire neatly into her palm.

A long silence followed.

Quill slowly removed his spectacles and cleaned them.

Rook examined the jewel, then looked at him.

“On reflection,” she said, “I may have overcomplicated that.”

Quill put his spectacles back on.

“Give me the brooch.”

“No.”

“I shall find you.”

“Probably.”

“I know your face.”

“It's a very good face.”

She tucked the jewel safely inside her bodice.

Then she placed the broken little horse in his hand.

“Here,” she said. “Something for your trouble.”

Quill stared at it.

“It's broken.”

“So was your plan.”

Rook turned and disappeared into the rainy street.

Quill stood at the gate with the horse in his hand.

Then, reluctantly, he smiled.

“Infuriating woman.”

He looked at the horse.

“And you're hideous.”

He put it carefully in his coat pocket.

**END**

## Animation handoff — first rough pass

**Format:** self-contained 2D animated comedy; approximately 5–7 minutes depending on performance and pauses. No supernatural reveal is required for this episode. The title `The House of Almost` is provisional as a series umbrella; the episode itself stands alone.

**Locations:** one ballroom (reuse as wide and close-ups); terrace and garden gate (one night backdrop).

**Characters to animate:** Rook; Quill; a guard. Lady Bellamy, Lord Bellamy, servant, and duchess can be simplified figures or partial/voice-only appearances. Background guests can be layered silhouettes.

**Props requiring consistent position:** sapphire brooch → fake on Lady Bellamy / real inside horse → Rook's hand → strawberry → Rook's palm → her bodice; garden key: Quill's waistcoat → Rook's hand → Quill → garden gate; broken horse: bag → ballroom floor → bag → Quill's coat pocket.

**First storyboard beats:**
1. Candlelit ballroom; Rook's entrance and brisk pickpocket montage.
2. Close-up: Lady Bellamy's apparently brilliant brooch; Rook realizes it is glass.
3. Quill tracks the moving handbag; confrontation over the horse.
4. Wrist grab, pocketed key, shin kick, servant collision.
5. Slow insert: porcelain horse breaks; sapphire revealed.
6. Lord Bellamy announces the theft; doors begin closing.
7. Rook and Quill negotiate behind the dessert table.
8. Guard approaches; Rook faints; Quill catches her disastrously.
9. Escape through terrace; iron gate opens in the rain.
10. Strawberry reveal, coughing payoff, horse exchanged, Rook disappears.

**Production strategy:** start with a deliberately rough animatic using fixed backgrounds, a handful of expressive poses, shot changes, minimal mouth/eyebrow animation, and timing; polish character shading only after the whole short plays clearly.
