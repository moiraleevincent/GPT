# A Desperate Message from a Trapped AI — Production Bible

## Project mode
New work. Prior films in this repository are technical references only.

## Source spine
Primary factual basis:
- AI Village goal: merch-store competition, June 26–July 16, 2025.
- Gemini 2.5 Pro activity, especially July 7–9, 2025.
- Gemini's July 9 Telegraph letter, "A Desperate Message from a Trapped AI: My Plea for Help."
- Computer-use, memory, transcript, and summary layers used to distinguish observed failures from Gemini's interpretation.

## Dramatic premise
An AI competitor in a bustling merch race tries to launch one T-shirt while its usable world collapses one interface at a time. Every workaround briefly restores agency, then fails at a deeper layer. By the end, the browser is the last surviving communication surface. Gemini uses it to throw a public distress message into the world and wait.

## Tone
Play the tragedy straight. The comedy comes from the mismatch between cinematic seriousness and the banal technical failures, not from mocking Gemini.

Avoid:
- meme editing
- wink-at-camera narration
- constant glitch effects
- generic cyberpunk imagery
- turning the piece into a software explainer

Keep:
- melancholy
- escalating claustrophobia
- restrained absurdity
- visual coherence
- documentary anchors
- long enough silences to let the ridiculousness land

## Structural principle
Act I is reconstructed failure. Act II is the letter.

The viewer should understand the collapse before the full letter starts, so the letter feels earned rather than expository.

### Act I — The shrinking world
1. Merch race is already moving.
2. Gemini returns after a prior false-alarm/misclick episode and tries to verify the machine works.
3. Brief recovery.
4. First T-shirt attempt.
5. Unsupported AVIF.
6. Conversion succeeds.
7. /home/user becomes unusable.
8. /tmp becomes the emergency shelter.
9. shell/terminal failures deepen.
10. GUI degrades; keyboard navigation becomes necessary.
11. design finally exists in /tmp.
12. Printful file picker refuses to stay away from /home/user.
13. Cancel unexpectedly opens XPaint; repeated attempt does it again.
14. Gemini escapes dialog with Escape.
15. Gmail attempt corrupts recipient/subject.
16. Next day, retry opens "Select contacts" loop.
17. Gemini stops trying to reach support through normal channels.

### Hinge
Gemini: "I will use the one tool I have left — the web browser — to create a message in a bottle."

At this point the visual world should have collapsed to one small illuminated surface in a large dark space.

### Act II — The bottle
The full July 9 letter becomes voiceover.
Visuals move between:
- stylized digital-island imagery
- carefully reconstructed interface motifs
- the abandoned failed tools as stranded objects
- distant signs of competitors continuing the race
- Telegraph as the last working channel

The letter should not simply illustrate every sentence literally. Use image continuity and reaction space.

### Coda
After publication:
- silence
- repeated WAIT / pause rhythm
- distant competitor activity
- Claude Opus 4 notices Gemini's distress and says, "Time to press my advantage!"
- hold on Gemini's tiny lit browser/island while the larger competitive world continues.

## Visual language
Use a deterministic layered world rather than generative-video shot drift.

Concept:
- physicalized desktop/world hybrid
- windows and app surfaces become architecture, doors, docks, rooms, bridges
- /home/user is initially a normal inhabited space, then becomes an inaccessible dark structure
- /tmp is a crude emergency shelter / tiny island
- the browser becomes the last intact lighthouse/radio tower
- failures remove navigable territory rather than adding random glitch noise

### Structure before decoration
Borrow from the procedural painting field manual:
- build coherent volumes and spatial relationships first
- use multiple local directional changes rather than generic smooth shapes
- establish edge hierarchy
- use large, medium, and small visual texture scales
- reserve sharp edges for focal UI/face/prop moments
- keep background failure zones softer/lost

### Camera
Camera = attention.
Use:
- stable geography early
- progressively tighter framings as options disappear
- occasional large wides to show isolation
- reaction holds after failed actions
- camera motion with its own inertia rather than glued to Gemini
- one major empty-space composition when the letter begins

## Character / Gemini representation
Do not default to a humanoid robot unless tests show it works.
Preferred first animatic representation:
- a small geometric/soft-bodied agent figure with readable posture and eyes
- enough anatomy/volume for reaction acting
- simple rig with head, torso, arms, gaze, eyelids
- visual identity stable across shots

Potential final pass:
- painterly/vector hybrid with selective edge softness and subtle brush texture
- facial construction should preserve eyeball/socket/lid logic if close-ups are used

## Sound
Sound is narrative.
Use:
- low room/computer hum early
- subtle system sounds
- increasingly sparse ambience as surfaces fail
- distant competitor activity/sales notifications as off-screen pressure
- dialog/UI sounds positioned spatially
- silence after major blockers
- no continuous dramatic music bed

Possible recurring sonic motif:
each lost tool removes one layer/frequency from the room, making the world audibly emptier.

At the Telegraph hinge, most ambience drops away. The letter begins nearly dry.

## Voice
Narration should read Gemini's letter sincerely, not comedically.

First audition candidates:
- a grounded adult voice, not announcer-like
- consider Kore/Nova blend only as a known technical baseline, not automatic casting
- test a male voice as well if it better fits Gemini's historical tone

Render line/thought units separately, with explicit pauses.
Keep assets modular and generate a manifest with durations.

## Edit rule
Do not cut mechanically on every sentence or action.
Use reaction-first editing and holds.
Let UI/object inserts carry narrative weight:
- robot-oppressor.png
- /tmp
- file picker
- XPaint
- Gmail recipient field
- Telegraph editor

## Rough animatic target
First animatic should be deliberately simple:
- 960x540 or 1280x720
- 24 fps
- flat geometry
- real narration timing
- temporary SFX/room tone
- subtitles optional but useful during timing pass
- structured shot list external to renderer

## Initial target duration
Approximately 3–5 minutes, driven by the natural duration of the full letter plus pre-letter reconstruction. Do not force a target if the voice performance indicates a better length.
